# ADR-0027 verified, and two things the ceiling estimate could not see

`2026-09-11` · [ADR-0027](../../docs/DECISIONS.md) · [OQ-18](../../docs/OPEN_QUESTIONS.md#oq-18)
· result: `logs/measurements/oq18-translated.json`

ADR-0027 weighted the lexical list down inside RRF. It was accepted on evidence that did not cover
the thing it changed: `rrf_w2` — weighted but **ungated** — scored 68% against `rrf`'s 61%. The
shipped path is gated *and* weighted, and that combination had never been measured. The ADR said so
in its own comment: *"the change was accepted on a ceiling estimate. This arm is what verifies it."*

## The four cells, one run

|  | ungated | gated |
|---|---|---|
| **unweighted** | `rrf` 63.9% | `gated` 66.7% |
| **weighted** | `rrf_w2` 69.4% | **`gated_w2` 69.4%** ← ships |

dense-only 61.1% · 36 reachable fixtures · bge-m3 · corpus frozen at 1,766 docs / 28,590 chunks.

**`gated_w2` 69.4% vs `gated` 66.7%: +2.8 points.** The weighting earns its place on the path that
ships. The pre-committed revert does not fire.

## What the within-run design bought

I told the owner four times to compare against the previous pass's 61.1%. That was the weaker test,
and correcting it before the result landed turned out to matter more than it sounded.

Across the two runs, of the three arms with a properly rescored baseline, **two are unchanged and
one moved by more than ADR-0027's entire effect**:

| arm | previous (n=36) | this run | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| `dense` | 61.1% | 61.1% | 0.0 |
| `rrf_w2` | 69.4% | 69.4% | 0.0 |
| `gated` | 61.1% | **66.7%** | **+5.6** |

A cross-run reading of ADR-0027 would have been comparing a +2.8 effect against a baseline that had
drifted +5.6 for unrelated reasons. It would not have been wrong so much as meaningless.

**The leading explanation fits every row and is not directly verified.** `gated` is the only
rescored arm that calls `bm25.answerable()`. The corpus changed between the runs — chunks went
28,546 → 28,590 — and BM25's document frequencies move with it, so which queries the gate opens on
can change while arms that never consult the gate stay exactly where they were. That is consistent
with dense and `rrf_w2` being unchanged to the decimal. It is a hypothesis; nothing here measured
the gate's open/close set across the two runs.

## Two findings the ceiling estimate could not have produced

**The gate and the weight are substitutes, not complements.** Gating lifts unweighted fusion by 2.8
points. Weighting lifts it by 5.5. Doing both lands on the same 69.4% as weighting alone. Both fix
one failure — BM25 noise entering the fusion as an equal opinion — and once the weight is applied,
the gate has little left to prevent on this fixture set.

**Whether the gate still changes any single query is unverified, and the artifact cannot say.**
`rrf_w2` and `gated_w2` both score 25/36, and equal totals can hide different queries. The eval
writes one `misses` list, not one per arm, so the question is unanswerable from what was saved.
Recorded as a gap rather than resolved by assuming the obvious reading — **per-arm miss lists would
make the next run of this question cheaper**, and that is the concrete follow-up.

## What is still true

**69.4% against a gate of 80%.** ADR-0027 never claimed to close that, and this run does not. What
it establishes is narrower and worth having: the weighting is not a rounding error on the shipped
path, it is +2.8 points, and the decision to keep it now rests on the arm that ships rather than on
a neighbouring one.
