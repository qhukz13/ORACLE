# 2026-08-24 — OQ-02: `bge-m3` on the full corpus, and the gate that was scoring it

Closes [OQ-02](../../docs/OPEN_QUESTIONS.md#oq-02), open since 2026-08-22 and reopened the
same day it was first resolved. The scheduled `bge-m3` run finally produced artefacts.

**Answer: `bge-m3`, and only together with a fix to the fusion gate.** Measured on the
full corpus with the retrieval code as it shipped this morning, `bge-m3` **loses** to
`multilingual-e5-base` — 53% against 55%, and 32% against 36% on the cross-language
column that OQ-02 exists to decide. With the gate fixed it wins by six points overall and
eight on that column, at 61% and 44%.

The comparison was measuring the gate, not the model. That is the finding.

## Setup

Both indexes built by the shipped path (`scripts/index_knowledge.py --full --measure`)
over the same corpus on the same day, and measured by the same `measure()`:

```
Corpus      1,413 documents · 14,566 chunks · 885 of them Cyrillic (6%)
Fixtures    tests/fixtures/retrieval/cases.yaml — 38 cases, 25 of them Russian
Gate        recall@5 >= 80% · retrieval p95 < 400 ms
```

Both builds ran warm (~70% cache hit), so their wall-clock — 48.5 min for `bge-m3`
against 20.8 min for `e5-base` — is a 2.3x ratio, not the 3.4x of a cold build. The
idle-machine throughput figures from [2026-08-22](2026-08-22-oq02-embeddings.md) are
unchanged: 1.37 against 4.71 chunks/s.

## 1. The result, four ways

| embedding | fusion gate | recall@5 | crosslang | semantic | lexical | p95 |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `e5-base` | as shipped | **55%** | 36% | 90% | 100% | 271 ms |
| `e5-base` | fixed | **55%** | 36% | 90% | 100% | 260 ms |
| `bge-m3` | as shipped | 53% | 32% | 90% | 100% | **401 ms** (fails) |
| **`bge-m3`** | **fixed** | **61%** | **44%** | 90% | 100% | 332 ms |

Read the first and third rows and `bge-m3` is not worth 2.3x the build and 2x the memory.
Read the second and fourth and it is the largest single improvement available to this
system. Same corpus, same fixtures, same model weights, both days.

The third row also **fails the latency gate** at 401 ms. The gate fix buys 69 ms of that
back, because BM25 stops running on every query.

## 2. The gate opened on 38 questions out of 38

`discriminating_terms` decides whether BM25's thirty results are admitted to the fusion at
all, and [RAG.md §5](../../docs/RAG.md#5-hybrid-retrieval) describes its purpose plainly:
a Russian question against an English codebase has nothing for BM25 to answer with, so the
lexical list is dropped rather than fused. It never dropped one. Instrumented over the
fixture set, the gate admitted the lexical half for **all 38 cases, including all 25
Russian ones**.

What BM25 returned for them:

```
ru-command-approval: кто решает что надо показать человеку опасную команду перед запуском
  kept   кто df=2 · решает df=4 · опасную df=1 · команду df=1 · надо df=2
         показать df=2 · человеку df=1 · перед df=36 · запуском df=4
  BM25   GrowAMonster/reports/task_report.md · GrowAMonster/docs/UI_SYSTEM.md
         GrowAMonster/docs/ICONS.md · GrowAMonster/3d_models/README.md
  dense  Asterim/apps/server/src/services/ApprovalManager.ts   <- the answer
```

GrowAMonster is the one project in this corpus documented in Russian. Every Russian
question retrieved it, whatever the question was about.

## 3. What the 2026-08-22 fix got right, and where it stopped

[The denominator log](2026-08-22-fusion-denominator.md) diagnosed this exactly: document
frequency measures rarity in the corpus, not uninformativeness in the language. It scoped
Cyrillic terms' denominator to the Cyrillic sub-corpus, `как` went from "0.8% of
everything" to "10% of the Russian" and dropped out — and recall did not move at all. That
log recorded the null result honestly and drew the wrong conclusion from it: that the
remaining failures must therefore be the embedding.

Two things were underneath:

* **The words below `как` are no better.** 885 Cyrillic chunks means a 10% ceiling of 88,
  and `если` ("if", df=37) and `перед` ("before", df=36) sit comfortably under it. Sweeping
  the Cyrillic ratio from 10% down to **zero** — which leaves only the `MIN_DF_CEILING`
  floor of 5, so only genuinely rare Russian words survive — moves recall by nothing.
  Those rare words match GrowAMonster too.
* **`e5-base` was too weak for fusion to damage.** Fusion can only displace a correct
  dense hit that exists. The null result was real and specific to the model it was
  measured on.

The second point is the one worth keeping. RAG.md §5 already recorded that fusion was
worth +9 to `e5-base` and −5 to `e5-small`; the pattern was there and read as a curiosity
rather than as a rule. **The stronger the dense retriever, the more an unfiltered lexical
list costs.** So the fusion policy cannot be measured independently of the embedding, and
neither can the embedding.

## 4. Per-case ranks, which is where it becomes unarguable

`bge-m3`, the same query run through the KNN alone and through the full pipeline.

| case | dense | fused | | case | dense | fused |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|---:|---:|
| ru-token-refresh | **3** | 9 | | ru-migrations | 15 | 28 |
| ru-feature-entitlement | 6 | 10 | | ru-command-approval | **4** | 6 |
| ru-kill-process-tree | 6 | 10 | | ru-workspace-permissions | 11 | 20 |
| ru-local-discovery | 7 | 12 | | ru-symbol-extraction | 8 | 14 |
| ru-bias-variance | 11 | 25 | | ru-learning-rate | **4** | 6 |

Every Russian case where the two differ, fusion made it worse, and three of them crossed
the top-5 cut on the way down. Dense-only recall on the cross-language column is **44%**
against the fused 32%.

It goes the other way on English, which is why the answer is not "delete the fusion":
`en-access-token-lifetime` (11 → 5) and `en-secrets-at-rest` (6 → 4) are cases BM25
rescues. Dense-only scores 70% on the semantic column against fusion's 90%.

## 5. Choosing the rule by measuring it

Every policy below over both finished indexes, dense candidates fetched once and reused,
so the only thing varying is what BM25 is allowed to see.

| policy | `bge-m3` all / crosslang | `e5-base` all / crosslang | gate opens |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| dense only, no BM25 ever | 55% / 44% | 47% / 36% | 0/38 |
| **as shipped** | 53% / 32% | 55% / 36% | 38/38 |
| Cyrillic ceiling to 0 (rare words only) | 53% / 32% | 53% / 32% | 38/38 |
| as shipped + at least 2 terms | 53% / 32% | 55% / 36% | 36/38 |
| majority script only | 58% / 40% | 53% / 32% | 16/38 |
| majority script + at least 2 terms | 61% / 44% | 55% / 36% | 11/38 |
| **majority script + >=40% of the question** | **61% / 44%** | **55% / 36%** | 11/38 |

Two rules, and both are needed:

**A term in a script the corpus is not written in is dropped, at any frequency.** Not
"ignore Cyrillic" — the test is *minority*, so a Russian-majority corpus gates out Latin
instead, and there is a test that builds one. This supersedes the per-script denominator
rather than extending it: once minority terms are gone, every surviving term is measured
against the whole corpus again, and the sub-corpus denominator has nothing left to scope.

**The surviving terms must cover at least 40% of the question's answerable terms.** The
case is `ru-token-refresh`: a Russian sentence containing the single Latin word `refresh`,
which passed the script rule on its own, opened the gate, and cost that case its top-5
slot under *both* embeddings. A one-term OR query ranked by BM25 is close to a
document-frequency ordering, and RRF admits it as a peer opinion. 30%, 40% and 50% score
identically — a plateau, not a fitted point — and 60% starts dropping English questions
BM25 does answer. Terms absent from the corpus are excluded from the denominator, because
a word BM25 cannot answer is not a word BM25 declined to answer: without that, one typo or
one novel identifier would close the gate on a question the lexical half handles well.

A **bare identifier** query is why this is a share and not a count. `PairingService` on its
own is 100% of its question and still opens the gate — which is the case §5 says the
lexical half exists for.

The `e5-base` column is the reason both rules ship together: the script rule alone costs
`e5-base` two points, and the coverage rule gives them back. **No configuration regresses**,
and `bge-m3` gains eight on the column that matters.

## 6. A test reversed, on evidence

`test_a_rare_russian_word_still_counts` asserted the opposite of what now ships. Its
reasoning was sound — a term in one document out of ten is exactly the evidence the
lexical half exists to supply — and the measurement refutes its premise: in a corpus that
is 6% Cyrillic, a rare Russian word is rare *because the corpus is not in that language*,
so what it matches is the unrelated Russian project rather than the subject of the
question. It is now `test_even_a_rare_minority_script_word_is_dropped`, and its docstring
says which assertion it used to make and why it reversed.

## 7. A quieter confirmation

The fixture file is itself a corpus document, and `measure()` discards top-5 slots it
takes for its own questions. That count went from **29 to 2**. It was being retrieved
lexically, by the Russian words in the questions it contains — the same mechanism, showing
up in the instrumentation rather than in the score.

## 8. What is still broken, and is not a model problem

**Neither configuration comes close to the 80% recall gate.** 61% is the best number this
system has produced and it is nineteen points short.

Of 25 Russian cases, `bge-m3` still never gets **7** of them into the thirty candidates at
all: `ru-pairing-bruteforce`, `ru-air-gap`, `ru-terminal-reattach`, `ru-audit-trail`,
`ru-cross-validation`, `ru-nonlinearity`, `ru-model-degrades`. No reranker, no wider top-k
and no fusion change can reach a document that is not in the candidate set. Four of those
are AI-vault questions whose neighbours (`ru-finetuning`, `ru-learning-rate`) do land, so
the vault is indexed and reachable — this is not a collection that went missing.

That is the next experiment, and it is not "try another embedding". The two levers not yet
measured are **query translation** (embed a Russian question's English translation as a
second dense probe) and the **~20% of chunks that exceed the 512-token limit and are
silently truncated**, still carrying a `TO VERIFY` in `rag/chunking.py` since 2026-08-22.
