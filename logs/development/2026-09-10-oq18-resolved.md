# OQ-18 resolved: the mechanism reaches the ceiling, and the gate is still missed

**Date:** 2026-09-10 · **Run:** 2026-09-09 20:51 → 2026-09-10 02:46, 18,682 chunks at 0.88 chunks/s ·
**Data:** `logs/measurements/oq18-translated.{json,txt}`

OQ-18 asked: *can a Russian question reach an English codebase well enough to meet the 80% gate?*

**Answers, in order of how much they change what we do:**

1. **Query translation works, and the cheap mechanism is as good as the expensive one.**
2. **The 80% gate is still missed** — the shipped path composes to **71%**, so the Phase 5 recall
   criterion stays unmet.
3. **No row of the arm table is the shipped path**, and reading one as such is a mistake this log
   made in its first version (§1b).
4. **The `gated` arm measures a gate the product replaced** two weeks before the run (§1c).
5. **One fixture is structurally unanswerable**, for a reason that generalises well past it.

## 1 · The mechanism reaches the ceiling

`dense_mt` and `dense_xl` are **identical where it counts**: 66% overall and 64% RU-only, both.

That comparison is the whole reason both arms exist. `_xl` embeds the *human* translations in the
fixture file — a number no running system can produce. `_mt` embeds what the resident `qwen3.5:0.8b`
actually emitted, recorded by `scripts/translate_fixtures.py`. P9-T2 had only the ceiling, and
shipping on it would have been shipping on a number the product cannot reach.

It turns out there is nothing to lose: **an 0.8b model's translation is as good as a human's, for
retrieval.** The two differ at r@1 (42% vs 37%) and r@10 (76% vs 79%) in opposite directions, which
at n=38 is noise, not a trend.

So there is **no headroom left in better translation** — a larger translator would buy nothing. That
is a useful negative result: it closes a direction rather than opening one.

**`Settings.translate_queries` stays `True`**, now confirmed by measurement rather than assumed:
translation lifts dense from 61% → 66% overall and, where it was aimed, **56% → 64% on Russian**.

## 1b · Correction: the shipped path is 71%, not 68% — and no row of the table shows it

**The first version of this log reported arm scores and called `rrf_w2`'s 68% "the best".** That was
wrong twice over, and `STRATEGIES`' own docstring says why:

> *None of these is "the shipped path" on its own … `retrieve()` picks per query: a Russian question
> loses its lexical terms to the script rule and runs `dense` (or `dense_mt`), while an English one
> runs `gated`. **The composition is done in the dev log from the miss lists.***

I skipped the composition. Doing it (`scratchpad/compose.py`, from the per-arm miss lists):

| | overall | RU (25) | EN (13) |
|---|---|---|---|
| dense | 60.5% | 56.0% | 69.2% |
| **dense_mt** | 65.8% | **64.0%** | 69.2% |
| dense_xl | 65.8% | 64.0% | 69.2% |
| rrf / gated | 60.5% | 48.0% | 84.6% |
| **rrf_w2** | 68.4% | 56.0% | **92.3%** |

**The shipped path — RU → `dense_mt`, EN → `gated` — scores 71.1%**, better than any single arm.
That is the number OQ-18 should report, and the reason no row shows it is that each arm is measured
across *both* languages while the product never uses one arm for both.

**The best available composition is 73.7%: RU → `dense_mt`, EN → `rrf_w2`.** A free +2.6 points from
changing only which fusion the English path uses. `dense_mt` and `dense_xl` compose identically, so
the mechanism-equals-ceiling finding holds at the composition level too.

**Two caveats, because this is derived rather than run.** `rrf_w2` was measured *ungated*, and
production gates its input; RAG.md §5 records the real gate opening on 11 of 38 queries, so a couple
of English fixtures would take the `dense` path instead and 73.7% is a ceiling on that change, not a
promise. And weighting RRF is exactly what [RAG.md §5](../../docs/RAG.md#5-hybrid-retrieval)
deliberately refused — *"tuning RRF's weights would have forfeited the property the algorithm was
chosen for"* — so this is evidence for reopening that decision with an ADR, not a licence to change
it quietly.

## 1c · The `gated` arm does not measure the gate the product ships

`gated` scores **identically** to plain `rrf` on all three columns. That is not a coincidence and it
is not the gate working: it is the gate never closing. If it had closed for even one query the RU
column would differ, because the fallback is `dense`.

The eval's gate is `BM25.answerable()` — true when **any** query term sits in fewer than 10% of
documents. A Russian question like *"где хранится секрет jwt"* carries the borrowed Latin `jwt`,
which is discriminating, so the gate opens and unweighted RRF admits thirty noisy BM25 results as an
equal second opinion.

**Production does not do this.** `rag/retrieval.py:discriminating_terms` applies two further rules,
both `MEASURED 2026-08-24` in RAG.md §5: minority-script terms are dropped at any frequency, and the
survivors must cover **≥40%** of the question's answerable terms. Those rules take the gate *"from
opening on 38 queries out of 38 to 11"* and are worth +8 recall / +12 cross-language.

So the eval's `gated` column measures a gate the product replaced two weeks before this run.

**Ported 2026-09-10, and measured on the real corpus:** the gate now opens on **13 of 38** fixtures
instead of 38 of 38 — **0 of the 25 Russian**, 13 of 13 English. RAG.md §5 records production going
to 11 of 38; the two-query difference is that production counts document frequency through the live
FTS index while the eval counts it through its own postings over a freshly-chunked corpus, so a
small drift is expected and a *large* one would not be. The important half is exact: every Russian
query now closes the gate, and English still fuses, so the lexical half keeps its job.

`tests/test_eval_gate_matches_production.py` pins the constants together. It deliberately asserts no
recall number — only that the two definitions cannot silently diverge again, which is the failure
that actually happened.

**What that implies for this run's table, derivable without re-running:** with the gate closed on
every Russian query, `gated` becomes `dense` for RU (56%, 14/25) and `rrf` for EN (84.6%, 11/13) —
**65.8%**, not the 60.5% printed. The shipped composition is unchanged at 71.1%, because it already
routes Russian through `dense_mt`. The arm was understating itself, not the product.

## 2 · The gate is still missed

The composed shipped path is **71%** against **80%**, and the best composition available without
reopening a decision is **73.7%**. The 2026-08-24 baseline was 61%, so the gap narrowed from 19
points to 9 — real progress, and not enough.

**Phase 5's recall criterion remains unmet, and OQ-18's own instruction stands: say so rather than
moving the gate to where the numbers already are.**

## 3 · Why fusion and translation pull against each other

**Naive fusion actively hurts translation.** `rrf_mt` (58%) is worse than `dense_mt` (66%) *and*
worse than plain `dense` (61%). The reason is in the breakdown:

| arm | crosslang r@5 | semantic r@5 | lexical r@5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| dense | 0.56 | 0.60 | 1.00 |
| hybrid | 0.48 | 0.80 | 1.00 |
| lexical | **0.00** | 0.70 | 1.00 |

**BM25 contributes exactly zero on cross-language queries** — obviously, a Russian question shares no
terms with English source. So fusing it into a good dense ranking can only dilute it. Hybrid buys a
lot on *semantic* queries (0.60 → 0.80) and pays for it on *crosslang* (0.56 → 0.48).

That is an argument for **language-aware fusion** — which the product already has, in
`discriminating_terms`' script rule, and which the eval's `gated` arm does not model (§1c). It is
also why the winning composition sends Russian down `dense_mt` and English down a fusion: the two
languages want different retrievers, and any single arm has to average over that.

**A `rrf_w2_mt` arm is still unmeasured** — weighted fusion over the *translated* probe. It is the
one combination the composition cannot be derived for, because it is a new ranking rather than a
choice between existing ones. Re-scoring needs the corpus to match the cached forward pass in
`D:/ORACLE/scratch/oq18-vectors-bge-m3.npz`, and **the tree has moved since**, so it now costs the
full six hours rather than seconds. Worth doing on the next run, not on its own.

## 4 · `en-relay-dockerfile` is unanswerable by construction

It misses in **every single arm** — dense, rrf, rrf_w2, gated, dense_xl, rrf_xl, dense_mt, rrf_mt.
That is not a ranking failure, and unlike `en-relay-secret-rotation` (fixed yesterday) the file is
present: `C:/Projects/Asterim/Dockerfile.relay` exists, is indexed, and the run printed *"all 38
fixture sources present"*.

The query is *"where do we configure the relay Dockerfile"*. Both halves of hybrid retrieval are
structurally unable to answer it:

* **Dense cannot.** `classify()` maps anything starting with `Dockerfile` to `ContentKind.CONFIG`,
  and `CONFIG.embeddable` is `False` — RAG.md §2 never embeds config, because an embedding of a
  tsconfig matches everything. **The document has no vector and never will.**
* **Lexical cannot reach it by name.** `chunks_fts` declares `rel_path UNINDEXED`, and
  `identifiers()` explodes identifiers out of the chunk **text**, never the path. **A document's
  filename is not searchable at all.**

So the only route left is the file's prose, and "relay" appears across the whole Asterim relay
subsystem — far too unselective to rank a Dockerfile first.

**Decision: keep the fixture, and do not reclassify it.** Marking it `kind: lexical` would move the
failure into the lexical bucket (currently a clean 1.00) without making it findable, which is
cosmetics. It is a true negative and it should keep failing until the underlying gap is closed.

**The generalisation is the finding**: ORACLE cannot answer *"where is X configured"* for any config
file, and cannot find **any** document by its filename. That is bigger than one fixture and gets its
own question (OQ-26).

## What this run cost, and one caveat on its numbers

5.9 hours at 0.88 chunks/s, against **2.52 chunks/s** measured on 2026-08-29. The machine was not
idle: the eval got 13.65 of 24 cores, with ~6.7 taken by ordinary desktop apps, while ONNX Runtime
was configured `intra_op_num_threads=24`. The 2026-08-29 figure was taken at 04:00 on a machine
woken by `WakeToRun` with nobody using it.

**So `chunks_per_s` from this run is not comparable to the earlier one and should not be quoted as a
regression.** The recall figures are unaffected — they are a function of what was embedded, not how
fast.

Also not comparable across runs: the corpus grew from 18,153 to 27,967 chunks since the 2026-08-26
baseline, partly because ORACLE indexes itself and this project commits a lot. Arm-vs-arm comparison
within this run is sound; absolute recall against the older runs is not.
